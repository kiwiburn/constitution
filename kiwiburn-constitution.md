## Part 1 - Introductory rules

### 1.1 The Society

**1.1.1** The name of the society is Kiwiburn Incorporated (in this Constitution referred to as the ‘Society’).

**1.1.2** The Society was constituted by resolution, dated 1 May 2007.

**1.1.3** The Society is a New Zealand regional group, associated with, but not legally linked to the Burning Man Project.

### 1.2 Charitable status

**1.2.1** The Society is not and does not intend to be registered as a charitable entity under the Charities Act 2005.

### 1.3 Definitions

**1.3.1** In this Constitution, unless the context requires otherwise, the following words and phrases have the following meanings:

**1.3.2** ‘Act’ means the Incorporated Societies Act 2022 or any Act which replaces it (including amendments to it from time to time), and any regulations made under the Act or under any Act which replaces it.

**1.3.3** ‘Ad hoc change’ means the appointment by the Governance Committee of a Member as an Acting Governance Member under the Acting appointments rule in this Constitution.

**1.3.4** ‘Annual General Meeting’ means a meeting of the Members of the Society held once per year which, among other things, will receive and consider reports on the Society’s activities and finances.

**1.3.5** ‘Acting Governance Member’ means a Member appointed under the Acting appointments rule to temporarily fill a Governance Committee vacancy.

**1.3.6** ‘Chairperson’ means the Officer responsible for chairing General Meetings and Governance Committee meetings, and who leads the Governance Department.

**1.3.7** ‘Committee’ and ‘Governance Committee’ mean the Society’s governing body.

**1.3.8** ‘Community Department’ means the Department that supports year-round community engagement and ensures that community voices are represented within the Society.

**1.3.9** ‘Constitution’ means the rules in this document.

**1.3.10** ‘Department’ means the Governance Department, Event Delivery Department, or Community Department, or other organisation group as directed by the Governance Committee.

**1.3.11** ‘Department Coordination Hui’ means a regular meeting of Guild representatives within a Department for coordination, information-sharing, and collective problem-solving.

**1.3.12** ‘Dispute Panel’ means a panel of at least three Members appointed under the Dispute Resolution Policy to receive, investigate, and determine disputes. A Governance Committee Member must not sit on a Dispute Panel. If an independent Panel cannot reasonably be formed, the Society must appoint an independent external person.

**1.3.13** ‘Department Lead’ means the person appointed to lead a Department and represent, or nominate a representative of, that Department to the Governance Committee.

**1.3.14** ‘Event Delivery Department’ means the Department responsible for planning, preparing, delivering, and closing the annual Kiwiburn Festival.

**1.3.15** ‘General Meeting’ means either an Annual General Meeting or a Special General Meeting of the Members of the Society.

**1.3.16** ‘Governance Department’ means the Department responsible for the Society’s governance, strategy, stewardship, accountability, and oversight, led by the Governance Committee.

**1.3.17** ‘Guild’ means a self-organising group within a Department, focused on a shared purpose, with a clear remit and boundaries set by the Governance Committee.

**1.3.18** ‘Guild Lead’ means the person elected by a Guild to coordinate that Guild.

**1.3.19** ‘Interested Officer’ means an Officer who is interested in a Matter for any of the reasons set out in section 62 of the Act.

**1.3.20** ‘Interests Register’ means the register of interests of Officers, kept under this Constitution and as required by section 73 of the Act.

**1.3.21** ‘Matter’ means—

**1.3.22** the Society’s performance of its activities or exercise of its powers; or

**1.3.23** an arrangement, agreement, or contract (a transaction) made or entered into, or proposed to be entered into, by the Society.

**1.3.24** ‘Member’ means a person who has consented to become a Member of the Society and has been properly admitted to the Society who has not ceased to be a Member of the Society.

**1.3.25** ‘Notice’ means written notice sent by email, post, courier, or another electronic process authorised by the Governance Committee. A notice sent by email or other electronic process is treated as received on the next Working Day after sending, unless the sender receives an error message. A notice sent by post or courier is treated as received five Working Days after sending.

**1.3.26** ‘Officer’ means a natural person who is:

**1.3.27** a member of the Governance Committee, or

**1.3.28** occupying a position in the Society that allows them to exercise significant influence over the management or administration of the Society, including any Chief Executive or Treasurer.

**1.3.29** ‘Register of Members’ means the register of Members kept under this Constitution as required by section 79 of the Act.

**1.3.30** ‘Secretary’ means the Officer responsible for the matters specifically noted in this Constitution.

**1.3.31** ‘Special General Meeting’ means a meeting of the Members, other than an Annual General Meeting, called for a specific purpose or purposes.

**1.3.32** ‘Treasurer’ means the Officer responsible for the matters specifically noted in this Constitution.

**1.3.33** ‘Working Days’ mean as defined in the Legislation Act 2019. Examples of days that are not Working Days include, but are not limited to, the following — a Saturday, a Sunday, Waitangi Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, ANZAC Day, the Sovereign’s birthday, Te Rā Aro ki a Matariki/Matariki Observance Day, and Labour Day.

### 1.4 Purposes

**1.4.1** The primary purposes of the Society are to—

- Promote the Ten Principles of Burning Man. Appendix 1 forms part of this Constitution and sets out the Ten Principles of Burning Man for the purposes of this Constitution.
- Provide the organisational framework, support, and functions necessary to promote and give effect to those principles.
- Give effect to those principles, through the organisation and delivery of an annual festival.
- Support year-round community engagement, creative projects, education, volunteer development, and activities that advance the Society’s purposes.
- Conduct the Society’s activities in an environmentally sustainable manner and foster respectful relationships with host communities, mana whenua, and relevant public agencies.
- Promote community interaction and participation in creative endeavours by its members, in the interests of social welfare and the improvement of members’ conditions of life.
- Invite all members of the public to attend the annual festival and to apply for membership of the Society.

**1.4.2** The Society must not operate for the purpose of, or with the effect of—

- distributing, any gain, profit, surplus, dividend, or other similar financial benefit to any of its Members (whether in money or in kind); or
- having capital that is divided into shares or stock held by its Members; or
- holding, property in which its members have a disposable interest (whether directly, or in the form of shares or stock in the capital of the society or otherwise).

**1.4.3** But the Society will not operate for the financial gain of Members simply if the Society—

- engages in trade,
- pays a Member for matters that are incidental to the purposes of the Society, and the Member is a not-for-profit entity,
- distributes funds to a Member to further the purposes of the Society, and the Member—
- is a not-for-profit entity, and
- is affiliated or closely related to the Society, and
- has the same, or substantially the same, purposes as those of the Society.
- reimburses a Member for reasonable expenses legitimately incurred on behalf of the Society or while pursuing the Society’s purposes,
- provides benefits to members of the public or of a class of the public and those persons include Members or their families,
- provides benefits to Members or their families to alleviate hardship,
- provides educational scholarships or grants to Members or their families,
- pays a Member a salary or wages or other payments for services to the Society on arm’s length terms (terms reasonable in the circumstances if the parties were connected or related only by the transaction in question, each acting independently, and each acting in its own best interests; or are terms less favourable to the Member than those terms and the payment for services, or other transaction, does not include any share of a gain, profit, or surplus, percentage of revenue, or other reward in connection with any gain, profit, surplus, or revenue of the Society),
- provides a Member with incidental benefits (for example, trophies, prizes, or discounts on products or services) in accordance with the purposes of the Society.
- on removal of the Society from the Register of Incorporated Societies having its surplus assets distributed under subpart 5 of Part 5 of the Act to a Member that is a not-for-profit entity.

### 1.5 Act and Regulations

**1.5.1** Nothing in this Constitution authorises the Society to do anything which contravenes or is inconsistent with the Act, any regulations made under the Act, or any other legislation.

### 1.6 Restrictions on society powers

**1.6.1** The Society must not be carried on for the financial gain of any of its members.

**1.6.2** The Society’s capacity, rights, powers, and privileges are subject to the following restrictions (if any)—

**1.6.3** The Society may borrow money only in accordance with the borrowing rule in the Finances section of this Constitution.

**1.6.4** All purposes of the Society must be carried out exclusively within New Zealand.

### 1.7 Contact person

**1.7.1** The Society shall have at least 1 but no more than 3 contact person(s) whom the Registrar can contact when needed.

**1.7.2** The Society’s contact person must be:

**1.7.3** At least 18 years of age, and

**1.7.4** Ordinarily resident in New Zealand.

**1.7.5** A contact person can be appointed by the Governance Committee.

**1.7.6** Each contact person’s name must be provided to the Registrar of Incorporated Societies, along with their contact details, including:

**1.7.7** a physical address or an electronic address, and

**1.7.8** a telephone number.

**1.7.9** Any change in that contact person or that person’s name or contact details shall be advised to the Registrar of Incorporated Societies within 20 Working Days of that change occurring, or the Society becoming aware of the change.

## Part 2 - Members

### 2.1 Minimum number of members

**2.1.1** The Society shall maintain the minimum number of Members required by the Act.

### 2.2 Ordinary membership

**2.2.1** The only type of Member is an Ordinary Member.

**2.2.2** Membership occurs when a prospective Member pays the annual subscription or purchases a ticket for the annual Kiwiburn Festival and, at ticket checkout, makes an explicit application for membership and gives written consent to become a Member. The annual subscription is to be set by the Governance Committee and published to Members.

**2.2.3** Every prospective Member must provide the Secretary with their name, address, telephone number, and email address for the Register of Members and Society communications. The Society shall retain the membership application and consent recorded at ticket checkout or with the annual subscription.

**2.2.4** A person joining through payment of the annual subscription without purchasing a ticket must provide written consent to become a Member. All Members are financial Members; the Society has no non-financial membership class.

**2.2.5** Membership starts at ticket purchase or payment of the annual subscription and expires one year after it starts. A Member may cancel membership at any time by giving written notice to the Governance Committee.

**2.2.6** Membership arising from ticket purchase may be cancelled if the ticket is refunded or transferred before the Festival. Membership must not otherwise cease merely because the Member did not attend the Festival.

**2.2.7** Membership may be ended only as an outcome of the dispute-resolution procedure and only for material misconduct, a material breach of this Constitution or applicable bylaws, or serious conduct inconsistent with the Society’s purposes or safety obligations. Any decision to end membership requires a 75% vote of the Governance Committee Members entitled to decide it.

**2.2.8** Behavioural expectations for Members are set out in bylaws and codes of conduct. Those documents must be applied fairly and consistently with this Constitution and the dispute-resolution procedure.

**2.2.9** By paying the annual subscription or purchasing a Kiwiburn Festival ticket, a prospective Member agrees to comply with this Constitution and the applicable terms and conditions of membership and event participation.

## Part 3 - Meetings

### 3.1 Types of meeting

**3.1.1** Live Governance Committee meetings may be held in person, by telephone, or through an internet chat facility. They shall be held not less than once a month. Electronic Governance Committee decisions may occur by email or another secure electronic process throughout the year in accordance with this Constitution.

**3.1.2** Each Department must hold regular Department Coordination Hui, attended by a representative of each Guild in that Department. Department Coordination Hui are for coordination and do not displace the authority of the Governance Committee or the operational autonomy of Guilds within their approved remits.

**3.1.3** A Special General Meeting may be called by the Governance Committee or on written request from at least 15 Ordinary Members. The request must state the business proposed for the meeting. The Governance Committee must issue notice of the meeting within 10 Working Days after receiving a valid request. A Special General Meeting may be held in person or through an internet facility.

### 3.2 Annual General Meetings

**3.2.1** An Annual General Meeting shall be held once each year between 1 June and 31 August, at a time and place determined by the Governance Committee. Any Member may attend, speak, and vote. The quorum is 20.

**3.2.2** The purposes of an Annual General Meeting are to receive an annual report on the Society’s operations and affairs for the most recently completed financial year; receive the Society’s financial statements for that year; receive a summary of interests disclosed by Officers during that year; receive the Chairperson’s, Community Department’s, Event Delivery Department’s, and Festival Afterburn reports; confirm Governance Committee appointments made since the preceding Annual General Meeting; consider properly notified challenges to Governance Committee positions; and consider motions submitted in accordance with this Constitution.

### 3.3 Governance Committee meetings

**3.3.1** Governance Committee meetings consider and decide matters reserved to governance under this Constitution, including strategy, risk, finance, policy, appointments, delegations, and accountability. Any Member may attend unless the Governance Committee reasonably resolves that all or part of the meeting must be confidential to protect privacy, legal privilege, safety, employment matters, commercial interests, or an active complaint. Only Governance Committee Members may vote. The quorum is a majority of current Governance Committee Members, with a minimum of three.

**3.3.2** The Chairperson shall chair all meetings. In the Chairperson's absence, the Governance Committee shall elect another Governance Committee Member as acting Chairperson. The Chairperson has a deliberative vote and, in the event of an equality of votes, a casting vote.

**3.3.3** Decisions are made by open vote at Governance Committee meetings, whether spoken or in writing by Governance Committee Members attending live or electronic meetings. A decision passes when a simple majority of the Governance Committee Members entitled to decide it agree, unless this Constitution requires another threshold.

**3.3.4** Governance Committee-only votes may take place by email or another secure electronic process established for that purpose. An electronic resolution must state the decision, give all eligible Governance Committee Members a reasonable opportunity to participate, record each vote, and be entered into the minutes. Feedback from Ordinary Members may be sought through the Kiwiburn internet community or another suitable channel.

**3.3.5** The Secretary shall give seven days' notice and a provisional agenda for live Governance Committee meetings, and keep their records. Approved minutes and a decision register must be made available to Members, except information properly withheld for the reasons stated above. The Society is not required to publish the full content of discussions, personal information, or confidential material.

### 3.4 Special General Meetings

**3.4.1** A Special General Meeting considers matters requiring input from Members as a whole. The purpose must be stated in the written request calling the meeting. The quorum is 20, and Ordinary Members may vote.

### 3.5 Notice, motions, and minutes

**3.5.1** The Governance Committee shall give every Member at least 28 days’ written Notice of a General Meeting and its business, including the text of every proposed constitutional amendment. Failure by one or more Members to receive Notice does not invalidate the meeting or its business.

**3.5.2** All Members are financial Members and may speak or vote at General Meetings. Each Member has one vote. Voting may occur by voice, show of hands, or electronic means.

**3.5.3** A Member may submit a motion up to 21 days before an Annual General Meeting. A written request for a Special General Meeting must include the full text of each motion proposed for that meeting. The Governance Committee must provide the full text of properly notified motions to Members no less than 14 days before the meeting.

**3.5.4** General Meeting minutes must record the names of those attending and the result of every vote, and must be made available to all Members.

## Part 4 - Governance Committee and Departments

### 4.1 Governance Committee composition

**4.1.1** Kiwiburn Incorporated is managed by a Governance Committee of volunteers. The Governance Committee includes the administrative roles of Chairperson, Secretary, and Treasurer, and any other governance roles required from time to time. The Governance Committee must appoint those administrative roles from among its Members.

**4.1.2** The Governance Committee must have at least five members. Only Members of Kiwiburn Incorporated may be Governance Committee Members. Any Ordinary Member may express interest in a Governance Committee vacancy.

### 4.2 Separation of governance and operational leadership

**4.2.1** A Governance Committee Member must not, while holding office, hold or undertake a Department Lead role, a Guild Lead role, or another role with primary responsibility for directing day-to-day operations in the Event Delivery Department, Community Department, or another operational Department. The Chairperson may lead the Governance Department.

**4.2.2** This restriction does not prevent a Governance Committee Member from volunteering or contributing to the work of those Departments or their Guilds in a non-lead capacity. Emergency operational intervention is permitted only where necessary for safety, legal compliance, or material financial risk; it must be recorded in the decision register and end as soon as practicable.

**4.2.3** A person holding a role restricted by this clause may be appointed to the Governance Committee only after they have resigned from that role. A Governance Committee Member who accepts or undertakes a restricted role must resign from the Governance Committee.

### 4.3 Governance Committee functions and authority

**4.3.1** Subject to this Constitution, the Governance Committee is responsible for the Society's purpose, strategy, stewardship, legal compliance, risk, finance, policy, delegations, appointments, and accountability. It must gather and consider Member and community feedback in making governance decisions.

**4.3.2** The Governance Committee must not ordinarily direct the day-to-day operation of the Event Delivery Department, Community Department, or their Guilds. It must instead set clear purposes, boundaries, budgets, policies, and delegations, and hold those Departments accountable for delivery within them.

**4.3.3** Governance Committee decisions bind Kiwiburn Incorporated, subject to the matters reserved to Members by this Constitution.

### 4.4 Matters reserved to Members

**4.4.1** The following matters may be decided only by Members at a General Meeting: amending this Constitution; confirming Governance Committee appointments; approving a properly notified governance challenge; approving the sale, purchase, borrowing against, or other disposal of an important asset as defined in the Finances section; approving liquidation or removal from the Register; and any other matter expressly reserved to Members by this Constitution or the Act.

### 4.5 Departments

**4.5.1** The Society has at least three Departments: the Governance Department, the Event Delivery Department, and the Community Department.

**4.5.2** Each Department must have a Department Lead. The Chairperson is the Department Lead for the Governance Department. The Governance Committee appoints the Department Leads in accordance with this Constitution and the Delegations Framework.

**4.5.3** The Governance Department supports the work of the Governance Committee and the Society's governance, strategy, stewardship, accountability, and oversight.

**4.5.4** The Event Delivery Department plans, prepares, delivers, and closes the annual Kiwiburn Festival.

**4.5.5** The Community Department supports year-round community engagement and ensures that community voices are represented within the Society.

**4.5.6** Each Department Lead, or another representative nominated by that Department Lead, must report monthly to the Governance Committee on the Department's activities, material risks, decisions requiring escalation, and progress against its plan.

### 4.6 Guilds

**4.6.1** Departments are made up of Guilds: self-organising groups focused on a shared purpose. A Guild may build its own identity and culture, recruit its members, and elect its Guild Lead.

**4.6.2** The Governance Committee gives each Guild a clear purpose, boundaries, reporting relationship, and any financial or other delegation. Within those boundaries, Guilds have operational autonomy.

**4.6.3** A representative of each Guild must attend regular Department Coordination Hui for its Department. Department Leads must ensure that those hui provide a practical forum for coordination between Guilds and for issues to be escalated where necessary.

### 4.7 Delegations Framework

**4.7.1** The Governance Committee may delegate any of its powers or functions, except powers that must by law or this Constitution be exercised by the Governance Committee or Members, to:

- a Department or Guild;
- a committee or working group;
- an Officer;
- a Member;
- an employee or contractor; or
- another suitably qualified person or body.

**4.7.2** The Governance Committee must maintain a written Delegations Framework and review it at least annually. The Framework must state the authority delegated to Departments, Department Leads, Guilds, and other volunteers, including spending limits, contract authority, appointment authority, emergency powers, reporting requirements, and matters that must be referred to the Governance Committee.

**4.7.3** The Governance Committee may amend or revoke a delegation, provided that it acts fairly, gives reasonable notice where practicable, and does not undermine the operational autonomy granted to a Guild except where necessary for safety, legal compliance, financial prudence, or the Society's purposes.

## Part 5 - Officers

### 5.1 Qualifications of officers

**5.1.1** Every Officer must be a natural person who—

**5.1.2** has consented in writing to be an officer of the Society, and

**5.1.3** certifies that they are not disqualified from being elected or appointed or otherwise holding office as an Officer of the Society.

**5.1.4** Officers must not be disqualified under section 47(3) of the Act from being appointed or holding office as an Officer of the Society, namely—

**5.1.5** A person who is under 16 years of age.

**5.1.6** A person who is an undischarged bankrupt.

**5.1.7** A person who is prohibited from being a director or promoter of, or being concerned or taking part in the management of, an incorporated or unincorporated body under the Companies Act 1993, the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, or the Takeovers Act 1993, or any other similar legislation.

**5.1.8** A person who is disqualified from being a member of the governing body of a charitable entity under section 36C of the Charities Act 2005

**5.1.9** A person who has been convicted of any of the following, and has been sentenced for the offence, within the last 7 years—

**5.1.10** an offence under subpart 6 of Part 4 of the Act

**5.1.11** a crime involving dishonesty (within the meaning of section 2(1) of the Crimes Act 1961)

**5.1.12** an offence under section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994

**5.1.13** an offence under section 22(2) of the Act

**5.1.14** an offence, in a country other than New Zealand, that is substantially similar to an offence specified in subparagraphs (1) to (3)

**5.1.15** a money laundering offence or an offence relating to the financing of terrorism, whether in New Zealand or elsewhere

**5.1.16** A person subject to:

**5.1.17** a banning order under subpart 7 of Part 4 of the Act, or

**5.1.18** an order under section 108 of the Credit Contracts and Consumer Finance Act 2003, or

**5.1.19** a forfeiture order under the Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, or

**5.1.20** a property order made under the Protection of Personal and Property Rights Act 1988, or whose property is managed by a trustee corporation under section 32 of that Act.

**5.1.21** A person who is subject to an order that is substantially similar to an order referred to in the previous paragraph under a law of a country, State, or territory outside New Zealand that is a country, State, or territory prescribed by the regulations (if any) of the Act.

**5.1.22** A person who does not comply with any qualifications for officers contained in this Constitution.

**5.1.23** Prior to election or appointment as an Officer a person must—

**5.1.24** consent in writing to be an Officer, and

**5.1.25** certify in writing that they are not disqualified from being elected or appointed as an Officer either by this Constitution or the Act.

**5.1.26** Note that only a natural person may be an Officer and each certificate shall be retained in the Society’s records.

### 5.2 Officers' duties

**5.2.1** At all times each Officer:

**5.2.2** shall act in good faith and in what he or she believes to be the best interests of the Society,

**5.2.3** must exercise all powers for a proper purpose,

**5.2.4** must not act, or agree to the Society acting, in a manner that contravenes the Act or this Constitution,

**5.2.5** when exercising powers or performing duties as an Officer, must exercise the care and diligence that a reasonable person with the same responsibilities would exercise in the same circumstances taking into account, but without limitation:

**5.2.6** the nature of the Society,

**5.2.7** the nature of the decision, and

**5.2.8** the position of the Officer and the nature of the responsibilities undertaken by them

**5.2.9** must not agree to the activities of the Society being carried on in a manner likely to create a substantial risk of serious loss to the Society or to the Society’s creditors, or cause or allow the activities of the Society to be carried on in a manner likely to create a substantial risk of serious loss to the Society or to the Society’s creditors, and

**5.2.10** must not agree to the Society incurring an obligation unless he or she believes at that time on reasonable grounds that the Society will be able to perform the obligation when it is required to do so.

### 5.3 Appointment and terms of Governance Committee Members

**5.3.1** Ordinary Members may put themselves forward, or be invited to put themselves forward, for appointment to the Governance Committee. Each vacancy must be publicly notified to Members for at least 14 Working Days with the role description, eligibility criteria, skills sought, and selection process. The Governance Committee must assess candidates against the published role criteria. Every candidate must declare in writing that they consent to being a Governance Committee Member and confirm that they are not disqualified under section 47 of the Act.

**5.3.2** Governance Committee Members are appointed by a majority decision of the Governance Committee. Each appointment, including appointment to Chairperson, Secretary, or Treasurer, must be confirmed by Members through a separate resolution at the next Annual General Meeting. The Notice of meeting must identify each appointee and include a short candidate statement. If an appointment is not confirmed, the position becomes vacant at the close of that Annual General Meeting.

**5.3.3** The term of a Governance Committee Member is two years. Terms should, where practicable, be staggered to support continuity. There are no term limits: a volunteer may serve consecutive terms in the same role. A Governance Committee Member must maintain membership of the Society throughout their term.

### 5.4 Acting appointments (ad hoc changes)

**5.4.1** The Governance Committee may appoint a Member as an Acting Governance Member to fill a vacancy or to ensure that the Governance Committee can meet its minimum membership or quorum. This is an ad hoc change.

**5.4.2** An Acting Governance Member has the same rights, responsibilities, and Officer duties as another Governance Committee Member while acting. Their appointment ends when the vacancy is filled, when the Governance Committee ends the acting appointment, or at the next Annual General Meeting, unless Members confirm the appointment at that Annual General Meeting.

### 5.5 Governance challenges at a General Meeting

**5.5.1** A Governance Committee role may be challenged only at a General Meeting. A challenge must be notified in writing at least 21 days before the meeting, state the grounds for the challenge, and identify an eligible Member who is ready to take over the role if the challenge succeeds.

**5.5.2** The challenged Governance Committee Member must be given a fair opportunity to respond and must not take part in the Governance Committee's consideration of the challenge. Members may approve the challenge by a two-thirds majority of Members present and voting. If approved, the identified replacement takes office at the close of the meeting, subject to written consent and eligibility requirements.

### 5.6 Department and Guild roles

**5.6.1** The term of every Event Delivery Department role is one burn cycle, being one year. The term of every other role in the Society is two years. There are no term limits, and a volunteer may perform the same role consecutively.

**5.6.2** Terms provide points to review the role, document knowledge, support handovers, and give volunteers a clear off-ramp when they are ready to step back. The end of a term does not prevent a volunteer from being reappointed or re-elected for a further term.

**5.6.3** Department Leads are appointed in accordance with the Delegations Framework. Guild Leads are elected by their Guilds in a manner determined by the Guild, provided that it is fair, documented, and consistent with this Constitution and the Guild's remit.

### 5.7 Vacancy and resignation

**5.7.1** A Governance Committee Member may resign by giving written notice to the Governance Committee. If a Governance Committee Member is absent from two consecutive meetings without leave of absence, the Chairperson may declare that person's position vacant.

**5.7.2** The Governance Committee may declare a Governance Committee position vacant if the member no longer meets the eligibility requirements in this Constitution, including the separation of governance and operational leadership rule.

### 5.8 Return of Society property

**5.8.1** On resignation or the end of a role, a former Governance Committee Member, Department Lead, or Guild Lead must give the Society all Kiwiburn Incorporated documents and property within one month.

### 5.9 Conflicts of interest

**5.9.1** Officers must disclose and manage interests in accordance with the Act and the Interests Register.

**5.9.2** An Interested Officer must disclose the nature and extent of the interest to the Governance Committee as soon as practicable after becoming aware of it, and the disclosure must be entered in the Interests Register.

**5.9.3** An Interested Officer must not vote on, sign a document relating to, or otherwise make a decision on the Matter except to the extent permitted by the Act or this Constitution. The Officer may take part in discussion and be counted for quorum unless the Governance Committee decides otherwise.

**5.9.4** Other decision-makers must disclose actual or potential conflicts under the Conflict of Interest Policy. That Policy may set proportionate requirements for Departments and Guilds and must not unnecessarily prevent ordinary operational decisions.

## Part 6 - Records

### 6.1 Register of Members

**6.1.1** The Society shall keep an up-to-date Register of Members.

**6.1.2** For each current Member, the information contained in the Register of Members shall include —

**6.1.3** Their name, and

**6.1.4** The date on which they became a Member (if there is no record of the date they joined, this date will be recorded as ‘Unknown’), and

**6.1.5** Their contact details, including —

**6.1.6** A physical address or an electronic address, and

**6.1.7** A telephone number.

**6.1.8** The register will also include each Member's —

**6.1.9** email address (if any)

**6.1.10** Every current Member shall promptly advise the Society of any change of the Member’s contact details.

**6.1.11** The Society shall also keep a record of the former Members of the Society. For each Member who ceased to be a Member within the previous 7 years, the Society will record:

**6.1.12** The former Member's name, and

**6.1.13** The date the former Member ceased to be a Member.

### 6.2 Interests Register

**6.2.1** The Governance Committee shall at all times maintain an up-to-date Interests Register containing the interests disclosed by Officers.

### 6.3 Access to information for members

**6.3.1** A Member may at any time make a written request to the Society for information held by the Society.

**6.3.2** The request must specify the information sought in sufficient detail to enable the information to be identified.

**6.3.3** The Society must, within a reasonable time after receiving a request —

**6.3.4** provide the information, or

**6.3.5** agree to provide the information within a specified period, or

**6.3.6** agree to provide the information within a specified period if the Member pays a reasonable charge to the Society (which must be specified and explained) to meet the cost of providing the information, or

**6.3.7** refuse to provide the information, specifying the reasons for the refusal.

**6.3.8** Without limiting the reasons for which the Society may refuse to provide the information, the Society may refuse to provide the information if —

**6.3.9** withholding the information is necessary to protect the privacy of natural persons, including that of deceased natural persons, or

**6.3.10** the disclosure of the information would, or would be likely to, prejudice the commercial position of the Society or of any of its Members, or

**6.3.11** the disclosure of the information would, or would be likely to, prejudice the financial or commercial position of any other person, whether or not that person supplied the information to the Society, or

**6.3.12** the information is not relevant to the operation or affairs of the society, or

**6.3.13** withholding the information is necessary to maintain legal professional privilege, or

**6.3.14** the disclosure of the information would, or would be likely to, breach an enactment, or

**6.3.15** the burden to the Society in responding to the request is substantially disproportionate to any benefit that the Member (or any other person) will or may receive from the disclosure of the information, or

**6.3.16** the request for the information is frivolous or vexatious, or

**6.3.17** the request seeks information about a dispute or complaint which is or has been the subject of the procedures for resolving such matters under this Constitution and the Act.

**6.3.18** If the Society requires the Member to pay a charge for the information, the Member may withdraw the request, and must be treated as having done so unless, within 10 Working Days after receiving notification of the charge, the Member informs the Society —

**6.3.19** that the Member will pay the charge; or

**6.3.20** that the Member considers the charge to be unreasonable.

**6.3.21** Nothing in this rule limits Information Privacy Principle 6 of the Privacy Act 2020 relating to access to personal information.

## Part 7 - Finances

### 7.1 Use of money and other assets

**7.1.1** Kiwiburn Incorporated money and other assets may be used only for a purpose of the Society approved by the Governance Committee or under the Delegations Framework, and not for the sole personal benefit of any Member.

**7.1.2** Money may be borrowed by Kiwiburn Incorporated only if approved by a majority of Members present and voting at a duly called General Meeting.

**7.1.3** An important asset is an asset that the Society cannot carry out its purposes without, or that represents 10% or more of the Society’s net assets as shown in its most recent annual financial statements. The Society may purchase, sell, dispose of, or grant security over an important asset only with approval by a majority of Members present and voting at a General Meeting.

**7.1.4** The financial year begins on 1 April and ends on 31 March of the following year.

**7.1.5** Any income, benefit, or advantage must be applied to the purposes of the Society. A Member or person associated with a Member must disclose and manage any interest in a decision concerning payment to or on behalf of that person in accordance with the conflicts-of-interest rules. Any payment must be reasonable and consistent with an arm’s-length transaction at open-market value.

**7.1.6** This personal-benefit clause may be amended only if it is replaced with a clause that continues to prohibit private financial benefit except as permitted by law and this Constitution.

### 7.2 Accounting records

**7.2.1** The Governance Committee shall maintain bank accounts in the name of the Society and keep accounting records as required by the Act. The Delegations Framework or a Finance Policy must set the Society's banking authorities, approval limits, and financial controls.

## Part 8 - Dispute resolution

As allowed under sections 40 and 41 of the Act, the Society adopts the dispute-resolution procedures set out in the version of Schedule 2 of the Act, or its successors, that is in force when the dispute-resolution process starts. The meanings of dispute and complaint are those in section 38 of the Act and its successors.

### 8.1 Meanings of dispute and complaint

**8.1.1** A dispute is a disagreement or conflict involving the Society and/or its Members in relation to specific allegations set out below.

**8.1.2** The disagreement or conflict may be between any of the following persons—

**8.1.3** 2 or more Members

**8.1.4** 1 or more Members and the Society.

**8.1.5** The disagreement or conflict relates to any of the following allegations—

**8.1.6** a Member has engaged in misconduct

**8.1.7** a Member has breached, or is likely to breach, a duty under the Society's Constitution or bylaws or the Act

**8.1.8** the Society has breached, or is likely to breach, a duty under the Society's Constitution or bylaws or the Act

**8.1.9** a Member's rights or interests as a Member have been damaged or Member's rights or interests generally have been damaged.

### 8.2 How complaint is made

**8.2.1** A Member may make a complaint by giving written notice to the Secretary, who must refer it to the Dispute Panel. If the complaint concerns the Secretary, the notice may be given to the Chairperson. If the complaint concerns the Chairperson or a Governance Committee Member, the Society must appoint an independent external person to receive and manage the complaint.

**8.2.2** states that the Member is starting a procedure for resolving a dispute in accordance with the Society's Constitution; and

**8.2.3** sets out the allegation(s) to which the dispute relates and whom the allegation or allegations is or are against; and

**8.2.4** sets out any other information or allegations reasonably required by the Society.

**8.2.5** The Society may make a complaint involving an allegation against a Member by giving to the Member a notice in writing that—

**8.2.6** states that the Society is starting a procedure for resolving a dispute in accordance with the Society's Constitution; and

**8.2.7** sets out the allegation to which the dispute relates.

**8.2.8** The information setting out the allegations must be sufficiently detailed to ensure that a person against whom an allegation or allegations is made is fairly advised of the allegation or allegations concerning them, with sufficient details given to enable that person to prepare a response.

**8.2.9** A complaint may be made in any other reasonable manner permitted by the Society's Constitution.

**8.2.10** All Members are obliged to cooperate to resolve disputes efficiently, fairly, and with minimum disruption to the Society's activities.

**8.2.11** The complainant raising a dispute, and the Governance Committee, must consider and discuss whether a dispute may best be resolved through informal discussions, mediation, arbitration, or a tikanga-based practice. Where mediation or arbitration is agreed on, the parties will sign a suitable mediation or arbitration agreement.

### 8.3 Person who makes complaint has right to be heard

**8.3.1** A Member who makes a complaint has a right to be heard before the complaint is resolved or any outcome is determined.

**8.3.2** If the Society makes a complaint—

**8.3.3** the Society has a right to be heard before the complaint is resolved or any outcome is determined; and

**8.3.4** an Officer may exercise that right on behalf of the Society.

**8.3.5** Without limiting the manner in which the Member, or Society may be given the right to be heard, they must be taken to have been given the right if—

**8.3.6** they have a reasonable opportunity to be heard in writing or at an oral hearing (if one is held); and

**8.3.7** an oral hearing is held if the decision maker considers that an oral hearing is needed to ensure an adequate hearing; and

**8.3.8** an oral hearing (if any) is held before the decision maker; and

**8.3.9** the Member’s, or Society’s written or verbal statement or submissions (if any) are considered by the decision maker.

### 8.4 Person who is subject of complaint has right to be heard

**8.4.1** This clause applies if a complaint involves an allegation that a Member, or the Society (the ‘respondent’)—

**8.4.2** has engaged in misconduct; or

**8.4.3** has breached, or is likely to breach, a duty under the Society’s Constitution or bylaws or this Act; or

**8.4.4** has damaged the rights or interests of a Member or the rights or interests of Members generally.

**8.4.5** The respondent has a right to be heard before the complaint is resolved or any outcome is determined.

**8.4.6** If the respondent is the Society, an Officer may exercise the right on behalf of the Society.

**8.4.7** Without limiting the manner in which a respondent may be given a right to be heard, a respondent must be taken to have been given the right if—

**8.4.8** the respondent is fairly advised of all allegations concerning the respondent, with sufficient details and time given to enable the respondent to prepare a response; and

**8.4.9** the respondent has a reasonable opportunity to be heard in writing or at an oral hearing (if one is held); and

**8.4.10** an oral hearing is held if the decision maker considers that an oral hearing is needed to ensure an adequate hearing; and

**8.4.11** an oral hearing (if any) is held before the decision maker; and

**8.4.12** the respondent’s written statement or submissions (if any) are considered by the decision maker.

### 8.5 Investigating and determining dispute

**8.5.1** The Society must, as soon as is reasonably practicable after receiving or becoming aware of a complaint made in accordance with its Constitution, ensure that the dispute is investigated and determined.

**8.5.2** Disputes must be dealt with under the Constitution in a fair, efficient, and effective manner and in accordance with the provisions of the Act.

### 8.6 Society may decide not to proceed further with complaint

**8.6.1** Despite the ‘Investigating and determining dispute’ rule above, the Society may decide not to proceed further with a complaint if—

**8.6.2** the complaint is considered to be trivial; or

**8.6.3** the complaint does not appear to disclose or involve any allegation of the following kind:

**8.6.4** that a Member has engaged in material misconduct:

**8.6.5** that a Member, or the Society has materially breached, or is likely to materially breach, a duty under the Society’s Constitution or bylaws or the Act:

**8.6.6** that a Member’s rights or interests or Members’ rights or interests generally have been materially damaged:

**8.6.7** the complaint appears to be without foundation or there is no apparent evidence to support it; or

**8.6.8** the person who makes the complaint has an insignificant interest in the matter; or

**8.6.9** the conduct, incident, event, or issue giving rise to the complaint has already been investigated and dealt with under the Constitution; or

**8.6.10** there has been an undue delay in making the complaint.

### 8.7 Society may refer complaint

**8.7.1** The Society may refer a complaint to—

**8.7.2** the Dispute Panel or an external person to investigate and report; or

**8.7.3** the Dispute Panel, an arbitral tribunal, or an external person to investigate and make a decision.

**8.7.4** The Society may, with the consent of all parties to a complaint, refer the complaint to any type of consensual dispute resolution (for example, mediation, facilitation, or a tikanga-based practice).

### 8.8 Decision makers

**8.8.1** A person may not act as a decision maker in relation to a complaint if there are reasonable grounds to believe that the person may not be impartial or able to consider the matter without a predetermined view.

## Part 9 - Liquidation and removal from the register

### 9.1 Resolving to put society into liquidation

**9.1.1** The Society may be liquidated in accordance with the provisions of Part 5 of the Act.

**9.1.2** The Governance Committee shall give 30 Working Days written Notice to all Members of the proposed resolution to put the Society into liquidation.

**9.1.3** The Governance Committee shall also give written Notice to all Members of the General Meeting at which any such proposed resolution is to be considered. The Notice shall include all information as required by section 228(4) of the Act.

**9.1.4** Any resolution to put the Society into liquidation must be passed by a two-thirds majority of all Members present and voting.

### 9.2 Resolving to apply for removal from the register

**9.2.1** The Society may be removed from the Register of Incorporated Societies in accordance with the provisions of Part 5 of the Act.

**9.2.2** The Governance Committee shall give 30 Working Days written Notice to all Members of the proposed resolution to remove the Society from the Register of Incorporated Societies.

**9.2.3** The Governance Committee shall also give written Notice to all Members of the General Meeting at which any such proposed resolution is to be considered. The Notice shall include all information as required by section 228(4) of the Act.

**9.2.4** Any resolution to remove the Society from the Register of Incorporated Societies must be passed by a two-thirds majority of all Members present and voting.

### 9.3 Surplus assets

**9.3.1** If the Society is wound up, dissolved, liquidated, or removed from the Register of Incorporated Societies, any property remaining after payment of all debts and liabilities must not be paid or distributed among Members. It must instead be distributed within New Zealand to one or more not-for-profit entities with similar purposes, selected by Members at a General Meeting.

## Part 10 - Changing the rules of the Society

**10.1.1** The rules of the Society may be changed by a two-thirds majority of Members present and voting at an Annual General Meeting or Special General Meeting, provided that the full text of the proposed amendment is included in the Notice of meeting.

**10.1.2** An amendment that materially changes the Society’s primary purposes or surplus-assets clause requires a 75% majority of Members present and voting at a General Meeting.

**10.1.3** Any approved amendment must be notified to the Registrar of Incorporated Societies in the form and manner required by the Act and takes effect on registration.

## Appendix 1: The Ten Principles of Burning Man

### Civic Responsibility

We value civil society. Community members who organise events should assume responsibility for public welfare and endeavour to communicate civic responsibilities to participants. They must also assume responsibility for conducting events in accordance with national laws and local bylaws.

### Communal Effort

Our community values creative cooperation and collaboration. We strive to produce, promote and protect social networks, public spaces, works of art, and methods of communication that support such interaction.

### Decommodification

In order to preserve the spirit of gifting, our community seeks to create social environments that are unmediated by commercial sponsorships, transactions, or advertising. We stand ready to protect our culture from such exploitation. We resist the substitution of consumption for participatory experience.

### Gifting

Burning Man is devoted to acts of gift giving. The value of a gift is unconditional. Gifting does not contemplate a return or an exchange for something of equal value.

### Immediacy

Immediate experience is, in many ways, the most important touchstone of value in our culture. We seek to overcome barriers that stand between us and a recognition of our inner selves, the reality of those around us, participation in society, and contact with a natural world exceeding human powers. No idea can substitute for this experience.

### Leaving No Trace

Our community respects the environment. We are committed to leaving no physical trace of our activities wherever we gather. We clean up after ourselves and endeavour, whenever possible, to leave such places in a better state than when we found them.

### Participation

Our community is committed to a radically participatory ethic. We believe that transformative change, whether in the individual or in society, can occur only through the medium of deeply personal participation. We achieve being through doing. Everyone is invited to work. Everyone is invited to play. We make the world real through actions that open the heart.

### Radical Inclusion

Anyone may be a part of Burning Man. We welcome and respect the stranger. No prerequisites exist for participation in our community.

### Radical Self-expression

Radical self-expression arises from the unique gifts of the individual. No one other than the individual or a collaborating group can determine its content. It is offered as a gift to others. In this spirit, the giver should respect the rights and liberties of the recipient.

### Radical Self-reliance

Burning Man encourages the individual to discover, exercise and rely on their inner resources.
